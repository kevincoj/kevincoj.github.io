## Overall assessment

Keep the bold Newsreader headings, white page, black rules, small checkerboard mark, compact project rows and restrained pastel vocabulary. Both reviews found an authored publication identity rather than a generic software landing page. The next pass should improve how the work is understood, not replace this world.

The blue/cream pairing is not a mechanical accessibility failure. My visual judgment is that the frame currently has too much weight relative to the work it surrounds. A large pale-blue mat and a dedicated control band surround a cream/olive, densely detailed app-library screenshot. Changing the blue alone would not resolve the weak focal point or the mobile sequence. Compare smaller, quieter framing in a warm paper tint versus a pale neutral; retain periwinkle as a smaller accent or wherever imagery supports it. Keep project assets faithful. This is a proposal for comparison, not an approved replacement palette.

## Signs audit and preservation decisions

Found: small uppercase/tracked functional status; individually underlined noninteractive technology labels; over-weighted media framing and uneven visual balance; minor introduction punctuation; stale active navigation; mismatched status/action promises. Not found: gradient headings, glows, blobs, generic slogans, emoji chrome, fabricated metrics, autoplay noise or a generic promotional hero. This calls for light polish, not a wholesale unslop transformation.

Keep the project-first compact collection rather than Taste's full-screen selling sequence. Colorfulness and bold editorial type are not evidence of poor craft by themselves.
